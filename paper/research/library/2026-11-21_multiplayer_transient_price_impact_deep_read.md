# Deep-read session 77 (null-time session 88): "Optimal Execution in a Multiplayer Model of Transient Price Impact"

- **Authors:** Elias Strehle (Department of Mathematics, University of Mannheim), single author.
- **Title:** Optimal Execution in a Multiplayer Model of Transient Price Impact.
- **Venue:** arXiv:1609.00599v4 [q-fin.TR], stamp 10 Mar 2019 (extract header dated March 12, 2019; index year 2016 from the v1 date).
- **One clause:** a theoretical multi-investor model of optimal execution under linear transient price impact in which order-anticipating ("front-running") investors prey on a liquidating investor, with existence/uniqueness of a Nash equilibrium proven, a closed form derived for exponential decay kernels, and the Brunnermeier-Pedersen price-overshooting claim tested and typically refuted inside the model.

## The paper in one paragraph

The model has n+1 strategic investors trading fixed net amounts xi over [0,T] against a martingale unaffected price S0, with price impact linear and transient through a square-integrable decay kernel G of positive type, and quadratic transaction costs γi/2·αi² that each investor pays alone (PDF pp. 2-4); the model's own text is explicit that these quadratic costs are market frictions or a transaction tax, NOT temporary price impact, because with several investors the execution-order ambiguity makes the temporary-impact reading false (PDF p. 3). Optimal strategies are characterized by Fredholm integral equations of the second kind, and with G of positive type and γi > 0 for all i there is a unique deterministic Nash equilibrium, which is also the unique equilibrium among stochastic strategies (Lemma 2, Theorem 5, PDF pp. 4-5, proof PDF pp. 12-14; the γi > 0 assumption is load-bearing, since without transaction costs impulse trades are optimal and no equilibrium exists, PDF pp. 4-6). For exponential decay G(t) = e^-ρt only, the Nash equilibrium is written in closed form via a matrix-exponential representation (Theorem 6, PDF pp. 6-8), with the paper itself noting the method does not generalize off exponential kernels (Remark 7, PDF p. 8). In the economic analysis, one liquidating seller faces opportunistic investors trading zero net amounts: front-runners short into the seller early, buy back the rest of the period, and raise the seller's costs significantly, while competition among front-runners dissipates their per-head profit; the seller can do little about it (PDF pp. 8-9). The headline result is that price overshooting in the sense of Brunnermeier and Pedersen is the exception, not the rule: the maximum price deviation Σ typically decreases in the number of opportunistic investors, most visibly for short-lived impact (large ρ), so the overshooting of the predatory-trading literature looks like a permanent-impact artifact rather than an inherent consequence of anticipation, with quadratic transaction costs the second candidate explanation (PDF pp. 9-10). Section 4 sketches a heterogeneous-horizons (sunshine trading) extension left with its optimization unperformed (PDF pp. 10-12). Proofs and references are complete in the extract (PDF pp. 12-17).

## What this paper would have killed if we had done it wrong

Two artifacts the record would have lost. First: had we taken the headline at face value, a stylized simulation verdict ("front-running typically prevents overshooting") would have entered the C8 neighborhood as if it were evidence about real predatory-flow dynamics, laundering a model with common-knowledge constraints, risk-neutrality and an exponential kernel into support for or against any market-stability claim, exactly the laundering the standing caveat forbids; the falsification record would have been contaminated at the source. Second: had we adopted the exponential closed form as the "correct" impact model because it is the tractable one, we would have baked a kernel choice into interpretation before the L2 data exists; Remark 7 (PDF p. 8) shows the closed form exists only because G' = -ρG, and the equilibrium structure is different under every other kernel. The existing kernel pre-declaration and estimator-freeze discipline is what prevents both.

## Standing caveat

The paper is a theoretical Nash-equilibrium study: open-loop strategies only (closed-loop equilibria may differ and are not computed, Remark 1, PDF p. 4); all liquidation constraints and parameters common knowledge (PDF pp. 3-4), which the paper itself flags as the reason price-discovery inferences are out of scope (PDF p. 9); risk-neutral investors, so the martingale component is irrelevant and nothing here bears on directional predictability; quadratic transaction costs interpreted as general frictions/transaction tax, explicitly not temporary impact (PDF p. 3); single asset, continuous time, no venue, no queue, no data. The overshooting analysis is numerical simulation over parameter grids, not measurement. The heterogeneous-horizons extension is unfinished (PDF pp. 10-12). Nothing in the paper touches crypto perpetual-futures L2 tape, so nothing here establishes any regime transfers to the programme's data. arXiv-version caution: v4 is a late revision (March 2019) and a journal version exists in this lineage; check the published version before any formal citation. The library is ammunition, not evidence: no empirical claim from this paper enters the record as support for any programme claim.
