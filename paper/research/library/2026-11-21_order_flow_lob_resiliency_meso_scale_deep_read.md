- **Authors:** Kyle Bechler (UCSB), Michael Ludkovski (Department of Statistics and Applied Probability, UCSB, corresponding author).
- **Title:** Order Flows and Limit Order Book Resiliency on the Meso-Scale.
- **Venue:** arXiv:1708.02715v1 [q-fin.TR], stamp 9 Aug 2017 (text dated June 29, 2017; index year 2017).

## The paper in one paragraph

The paper proposes volume-based bucketing as the natural clock for the meso-scale between event-by-event microstructure and diffusion-limit macro models: buckets are cut at fixed executed market volume V (0.25%, 1%, 2% of ADV), which stabilizes the series, removes intraday seasonality, improves Gaussianity and normalizes limit-flow comparisons across activity levels; median bucket duration is roughly 170 seconds with dozens of trades and hundreds of limit orders per bucket (PDF pp. 6-9). Against this clock, mid-price change ΔP versus trade imbalance TI follows a persistent S-shaped nonlinearity, with flat or declining expected ΔP near TI = ±1, which the authors attribute to intelligent order placement conditioning on the book state (PDF pp. 10-13); a linear regression ΔP = α0 + α1 TI + α2 (VLB − VLA) restores near-linearity and raises R2 from about 0.3-0.4 to about 0.7, defining NetLiq = TI + β·VL with β (touch-limit-order price impact relative to market orders) stable at roughly 0.4-0.68 across assets and bucket sizes (PDF pp. 11-14, Tables 3-4). The limit flow response to TI is hockey-stick shaped per side: the active side is replenished less under one-sided pressure, and for some tickers the passive side "hibernates" too, so resilience is driven by one-sided flows, not aggregates; the authors state the causality direction between market and limit flow cannot be determined (PDF pp. 14-15). Variable-importance analysis across linear models, LASSO, MARS and random forests finds TI and the one-sided touch limit flows VLj the dominant predictors of ΔP; top-level depth D1 is statistically insignificant while deeper shape metrics (D2, the hypothetical execution cost PI over 3-5 levels, and the impact slope S over about 4 levels) carry the modulation, and the full model reaches R2 near 0.8 (PDF pp. 15-18, Table 6). Scarce liquidity SLj is defined as a residual indicator from the ΔP-vs-TI GAM fit at 1.5 standard deviations (about 6% of buckets), and logistic/RF models show it is driven by limit-flow asymmetry, reduced depth and high cancellation proportions, with time-of-day and slow-trading effects; the models are right 70-80% of the time when they predict SL at high probability but miss more than half of actual occurrences at a 4-5% base rate (PDF pp. 18-19, Table 7). Time-series observations: long-memory persistence in VLj over hours, no clustering in one-sided SL but positive autocorrelation in the combined indicator and a whiplash cross-correlation of opposite-signed abnormal moves (PDF pp. 19-20); VPIN as mean absolute trade imbalance over recent buckets (eq. 18) and an event-based flow-price correlation toxicity metric (eq. 19) both showed weak predictive power in the authors' regressions (PDF p. 21, Remark 6).

## Findings, graded (does any kill line move?)

NO kill line changes. NO new candidate sharpening.

1. [reinforcing, no new item] Clock choice as a dial that reaches the measured relations, not just their precision: volume bucketing is the paper's own instrument (PDF pp. 7-9) and the fitted quantities move with the bucket size V, R2 rising with V as microstructure noise averages out and β rising with V toward mechanical liquidity accounting (PDF pp. 12-14, Tables 3-4). This re-exhibits on the flow-clock side what the queued flow-clock scale-constant and volume-clock convention items already bind: any clock transformation a future test's null, comparator or reconstruction uses must be pre-declared and counted as a configuration, because the measured relation is not invariant to it. No new axis; nothing queued — the ground is already owned.

2. [interpretation caution] Taker-only clocks are structurally incomplete here, and this is the paper's sharpest relevance to T1's booked state: the S-shape disappears and R2 roughly doubles ONLY when the contemporaneous passive (touch limit) flow enters with a fitted weight of 50-70% of market-order impact (PDF pp. 11-14). The programme's aggTrades windows are taker prints only, the passive-withdrawal mechanism is invisible by the standing T1 constraint, and no taker proxy may be substituted. Reading discipline: a ΔP-versus-TI (or any taker-only flow) fit on crypto perp tape that finds nonlinearity or weak explanatory power CANNOT be read as absence of a linear flow-price structure, because on the equities meso-scale that structure only exists after adding the flow the taker clock cannot see. This sharpens C1-L2's motivation (the signal, if any, lives where taker-only data cannot fully represent it) and guards T1's interpretation; no queue change, the standing passive-invisibility constraint already covers the mechanism.

3. 4. [interpretation caution] Residual-threshold abnormality is in-sample by construction, and precision without recall flatters a low-base-rate predictor: SL is the residual of a GAM fitted over the SAME sample, cut at 1.5 StDev of that same sample (PDF p. 18); the predictive models are correct in 70-80% of high-probability predictions but identify fewer than half of actual occurrences at a 4-5% holdout base rate (PDF pp. 18-19, Table 7). Reading discipline for any future programme definition of an "abnormal move" or rare-state predictor: the conditioning fit window must be pre-declared and out-of-sample, and recall at the named base rate must be reported, or the predictor can look strong while missing most of the events. ## What this paper would have killed if we had done it wrong

First: had the programme ever fitted a ΔP-versus-taker-imbalance regression on crypto perp data and found a nonlinear, weakly-explaining relation, the unqualified conclusion "there is no linear flow-price structure here" could have entered the record as a C8/comparator refutation, when the paper's central mechanism is exactly that the linear structure appears only after including the passive flow that a taker-only clock cannot observe; the standing aggTrades passive-invisibility constraint is what prevented that inversion. ## Standing caveat

The scope is liquid, large-tick US equities on a single venue with one-tick spreads that barely move (PDF pp. 4, 8-9); the authors explicitly do not claim small-tick, illiquid or high-price assets (PDF p. 22) and nothing is crypto. All headline R2 values (Tables 3-4, section 4) are in-sample except the Table 7 holdout; PI is a counterfactual construct the paper itself warns is unrealizable in practice due to latency (Remark 1, PDF p. 5); the SL threshold is in-sample (PDF p. 18); the VM-VL causality is undetermined (PDF p. 14); the bucketing drops hidden-order executions.
