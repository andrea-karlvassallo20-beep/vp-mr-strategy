# 031 — Autoregressive Conditional Heteroscedasticity (Engle, 1982)

**Venue:** Econometrica | **Category:** Volatility & risk
**Status:** Synthesized from LLM internal knowledge (Paywalled paper).

## Summary
ARCH: conditional variance is a function of past squared
innovations, so volatility clusters. The innovation was to split
variance into unconditional (constant) and conditional
(time-varying, predictable) parts, estimated by ML with a
quasi-likelihood for the normal-ARCH case and robust extensions
(GARCH) following.

## The math that matters
$\epsilon_t = \sigma_t z_t$, $\sigma_t^2 = \omega + \sum_{i=1}^q
\alpha_i \epsilon_{t-i}^2$; log-likelihood
$\ell = -\frac12\sum_t \left(\log\sigma_t^2 + \epsilon_t^2/\sigma_t^2\right)$.

## Why it matters for the L2 project
The vol factor that killed the compression family (C1, C7) is an
ARCH-type object: quietness is partly predictable from past quiet.
Any "priming" claim must therefore beat $\sigma_t^2$'s own
forecast, which is the K1 kill line in statistical form.
