# 027 — The Likelihood Ratio Test Under Nonstandard Conditions (Hansen, 1992)

**Venue:** Journal of Applied Econometrics | **Category:** Regime switching & state-space
**Status:** Synthesized from LLM internal knowledge (Paywalled paper).

## Summary
Markov-switching models have parameters unidentified under the null
of no switching (nuisance parameters present only under the
alternative), so the standard chi-square likelihood-ratio
distribution fails. Hansen provides a computable bound on the
asymptotic distribution of the LR test under nonstandard
conditions, letting researchers test "is there regime switching at
all?" honestly.

## The math that matters
LR statistic $LR = 2(\ell_1 - \ell_0)$ has a nonstandard limit;
Hansen's procedure computes
$\sup\, \text{LR}(\theta)$ over the unidentified nuisance
parameters and gives an asymptotically valid p-value bound via
simulation of the score process.

## Why it matters for the L2 project
If C8 estimates regime states (Hawkes regimes, Primed states) and
then tests "does the regime matter?", the naive LR/chi-square test
is invalid for exactly Hansen's reason. Any state-existence claim
in the L2 family must use a bootstrap or Hansen-style bound.
