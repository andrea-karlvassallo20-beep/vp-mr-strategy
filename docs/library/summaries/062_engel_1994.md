# 062 — Can the Markov Switching Model Forecast Exchange Rates? (Engel, 1994)

**Venue:** Journal of International Economics | **Category:** Regime switching & state-space
**Status:** Synthesized from LLM internal knowledge (Paywalled paper).

## Summary
Tests whether Markov-switching models of exchange rates (with
regime-dependent drift and variance, à la Hamilton) forecast
better than random walk and single-state alternatives. Results
are mixed: regime models fit in-sample regularities (volatility
regimes, occasional drift) but beating the random walk
out of sample is hard — the classic Meese-Rogoff result largely
survives the regime treatment.

## The math that matters
Forecast comparison: MS model forecasts
$\hat r_{t+h} = \sum_j \Pr(s_{t+h}=j \mid y_{1:t})\,\mu_j$ vs
random walk; evaluated by OOS RMSE ratios and forecast
encompassing tests.

## Why it matters for the L2 project
The cautionary precedent for C8: a regime structure that is real
(in-sample) may still not forecast better out of sample. C8's
kill lines therefore demand OOS uplift over the single-state
benchmark, not evidence that regimes exist.
