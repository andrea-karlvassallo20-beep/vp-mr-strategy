# 004 — A Test for Superior Predictive Ability (Hansen, 2005)

**Venue:** Journal of Business & Economic Statistics | **Category:** Backtesting & FML
**Status:** Synthesized from LLM internal knowledge (Paywalled paper).

## Summary
Hansen refines White's Reality Check with two ideas: (1) recenter the
bootstrap by the sample estimate of the best rule's performance,
which tightens the test when the best rule is genuinely good; (2)
studentize the performance differentials by their standard errors so
noisy rules do not distort the null. The resulting SPA test has
higher power than the Reality Check under "poor" and "neutral"
scenarios where many rules are bad.

## The math that matters
Test statistic $T^{SPA} = \max\left(0,\ \max_m
\frac{\sqrt{n}\,\bar{d}_m}{\hat\omega_m}\right)$ with $\bar d_m$ the
mean performance differential of rule $m$ vs the benchmark and
$\hat\omega_m^2 = \text{Var}(\sqrt n\, \bar d_m)$; p-values from
stationary bootstrap with recentering $\bar d_m - \hat\omega_m
\max(0, \bar d^*)$.

## Why it matters for the L2 project
The project's multiplicity tax (kill line K3, DSR deflation) should
be computed with an SPA-style recentered bootstrap when the number of
L2 configurations tried is large — it is the modern, more powerful
form of the Reality Check.
