# 041 — The Myth of the Smart Money (Barber & Odean, 2000/2002)

**Venue:** Journal of Financial Economics (record to verify) | **Category:** Backtesting & FML
**Status:** Synthesized from LLM internal knowledge (Paywalled paper; catalogue notes title/venue to verify).

## Summary
The companion evidence to 010 from the same research program:
individual investor performance is systematically negative after
costs, and the apparent "smart money" of certain accounts does not
survive controls for luck, style exposure, and costs. Where 010
showed overtrading destroys returns, this line of work shows that
even the winners are mostly the right tail of noise.

## The math that matters
Performance attribution:
$R^{net}_i = \alpha_i + \beta_i' F - c\cdot\text{turnover}_i$, with
$\alpha_i$ indistinguishable from zero (or negative) after costs
for the vast majority of accounts; luck dominates the cross-section
of account performance.

## Why it matters for the L2 project
The base-rate prior for any account-level or strategy-level
"discovered edge": most apparent skill is the right tail of noise.
The project's placebo tests (era-shuffled labels) are the
operational form of this skepticism.
