# 039 — The Cross-Section of Volatility and Expected Returns (Ang, Hodrick, Xing & Zhang, 2006)

**Venue:** Journal of Finance | **Category:** Backtesting & FML
**Status:** Synthesized from LLM internal knowledge (Paywalled paper).

## Summary
The volatility anomaly: stocks with high idiosyncratic volatility
earn LOWER future returns — the opposite of any risk-based
prediction. Robust to many controls, and one of the hardest
anomalies for standard factor models to absorb.

## The math that matters
Portfolio sorts on idiosyncratic vol
$\sigma_i = \text{sd}(\epsilon_{it})$ from Fama-French residual
regressions; high-IVol quintile underperforms low-IVol quintile by
~1%/month; Fama-MacBeth $\gamma$ on $\sigma_i$ is negative.

## Why it matters for the L2 project
Volatility is not compensation — it is a characteristic, and a
negative one. The project's compression deaths are consistent with
this: "quiet" assets are not cheap options on future calm; if
anything the cross-section says the opposite. C8's macro state
must survive this precedent.
