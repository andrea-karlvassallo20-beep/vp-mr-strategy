# 043 — WATCH: Wasserstein Change Point Detection for High-Dimensional Time Series (2021)

**Venue:** IEEE Big Data | **Category:** Change-point & shift

## Summary
WATCH detects distributional change points online by comparing the
empirical distribution of a sliding window against a reference
window using the (first) Wasserstein distance, with a threshold
from the null distribution of the distance under stationarity. On
high-dimensional series it uses sliced/random projections to make
the Wasserstein computation tractable. Flagged as a research lead
for the project: change detection on L2 book-feature distributions.

## The math that matters
$W_1(\hat\mu_t, \hat\mu_{ref}) = \min_{\gamma \in \Pi(\hat\mu_t,
\hat\mu_{ref})} \int \|x-y\|\, d\gamma$; alarm when
$W_1 > q_{1-\alpha}$, the empirical $(1-\alpha)$-quantile of the
distance under stationary resampling; 1-D projections reduce
$W_1$ to the $\ell^1$ distance between sorted samples
$\sum_i |x_{(i)} - y_{(i)}|$.

## Why it matters for the L2 project
The designated change detector for the L2 family: book-state
changes (depth regime shifts, branching-ratio regime shifts) as
$W_1$ alarms on sliding windows of book features. The cheatsheet's
OT sections (011, 019) supply the computational core.
