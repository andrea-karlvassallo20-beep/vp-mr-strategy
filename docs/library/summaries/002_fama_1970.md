# 002 — Efficient Capital Markets: A Review of Theory and Empirical Work (Fama, 1970)

**Venue:** Journal of Finance | **Category:** Asset pricing & efficiency
**Status:** Synthesized from LLM internal knowledge (Paywalled paper).

## Summary
The foundational review that fixed the taxonomy: **weak form**
(prices reflect past price information), **semi-strong form** (prices
reflect all public information), **strong form** (prices reflect all
information, public and private). Fama formalizes efficiency as a
martingale/fair-game condition: $E[P_{t+1} \mid \Phi_t] = P_t$ (up to a
normal return for risk). Under the weak form, technical analysis is
vacuous; under the semi-strong form, event-study evidence shows prices
adjust rapidly to announcements, though the review already concedes
anomalous drift.

## The math that matters
The fair-game condition: $E[R_{t+1} \mid \Phi_t] - E[R_{t+1}] = 0$;
tests are joint tests of efficiency and of the model generating
$E[R_{t+1}]$.

## Why it matters for the L2 project
It defines the null the project is trying to kill: any L2 feature
(OFI, Hawkes branching ratio) claiming information about `y_hold`
must beat the fair game after costs, splits, and multiplicity
deflation — exactly what the C-series kill lines encode.
