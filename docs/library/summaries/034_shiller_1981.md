# 034 — Do Stock Prices Move Too Much to Be Justified by Subsequent Changes in Dividends? (Shiller, 1981)

**Venue:** American Economic Review | **Category:** Asset pricing & efficiency
**Status:** Synthesized from LLM internal knowledge (Paywalled paper).

## Summary
Excess volatility: the variance of market prices vastly exceeds the
variance of the discounted dividend stream they allegedly
capitalize. Prices move more than fundamentals justify — the
founding anomaly of behavioral finance, and the reason
"volatility" cannot be assumed to be information.

## The math that matters
Variance-bound test: under a present-value model,
$\text{Var}(P_t) \le \text{Var}(P_t^*)$ where $P_t^*$ is the
perfect-foresight price; Shiller showed the reverse by a wide
margin (with later corrections to the statistical treatment).

## Why it matters for the L2 project
Volatility is not pure information; much of it is endogenous
dynamics — exactly the Filimonov-Sornette claim that markets
self-excite. The Hawkes branching ratio is a mechanism-level
measurement of what Shiller documented at the aggregate level.
