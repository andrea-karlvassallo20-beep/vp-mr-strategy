# 001 — Efficient Capital Markets: II (Fama, 1991)

**Venue:** Journal of Finance | **Category:** Asset pricing & efficiency
**Status:** Synthesized from LLM internal knowledge (Paywalled paper).

## Summary
Fama's ten-year update of his 1970 review. The core contribution is the
**joint hypothesis problem**: any test of market efficiency is
simultaneously a test of the equilibrium pricing model used to define
"normal" returns. Anomalies (size, momentum, calendar effects) can be
read as market inefficiency *or* as misspecified risk models — the data
cannot distinguish the two. Fama reviews the predictability evidence:
returns are predictable from dividend yields, interest rates, and
lagged returns at horizons of a quarter to several years, but this
predictability is modest and confounded with time-varying expected
returns.

## The math that matters
Conditional expected returns vary with conditioning variables
$Z_t$: $E[R_{t+1} \mid Z_t] \ne E[R_{t+1}]$, but the proxy $Z_t$ is
itself model-dependent — there is no model-free test of efficiency.
