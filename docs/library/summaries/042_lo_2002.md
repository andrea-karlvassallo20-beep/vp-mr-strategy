# 042 — The Statistics of Sharpe Ratios (Lo, 2002)

**Venue:** Financial Analysts Journal | **Category:** Backtesting & FML
**Status:** Synthesized from LLM internal knowledge (Paywalled paper).

## Summary
Sharpe ratios are not comparable across strategies with different
return autocorrelation: annualizing by $\sqrt{12}$ or $\sqrt{252}$
is only valid under i.i.d. returns. Lo derives the correct
annualization factor as the ratio of the standard deviations of
annual vs per-period returns, which includes all autocovariances —
smoothing (e.g. trend-following) inflates naive Sharpe ratios
dramatically.

## The math that matters
$\text{SR}_{annual} = \frac{\mu_a}{\sigma_a}$ with
$\sigma_a^2 = \sigma^2\left(12 + 2\sum_{k=1}^{11}(12-k)\rho_k\right)$
for monthly returns with autocorrelations $\rho_k$; the i.i.d.
case reduces to $\sqrt{12}$.

## Why it matters for the L2 project
C8's performance readings on 4h-horizon labels are serially
correlated by construction (overlapping holds). Any Sharpe-type
annualization must use Lo's correction, or the kill-line thresholds
are miscalibrated by the autocorrelation factor.
