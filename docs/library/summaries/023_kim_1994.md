# 023 — Dynamic Linear Models with Markov-Switching (Kim, 1994)

**Venue:** Journal of Econometrics | **Category:** Regime switching & state-space
**Status:** Synthesized from LLM internal knowledge (Paywalled paper).

## Summary
Combines Markov switching with linear-Gaussian state-space models:
the observation equation's parameters (or the state dynamics) depend
on a hidden Markov chain, and the continuous state is integrated out
by an approximate Kalman filter per regime followed by a probability
collapse (Kim's filtering and smoothing algorithm). Makes
regime-switching tractable for models with latent continuous
components (e.g. time-varying coefficients, stochastic volatility).

## The math that matters
Approximation: $\Pr(s_t = j \mid y_{1:t}) \approx
\frac{f(y_t \mid s_t=j, y_{1:t-1})\,\sum_i p_{ij}\Pr(s_{t-1}=i \mid y_{1:t-1})}
{\sum_k f(y_t \mid s_t=k, y_{1:t-1})\,\sum_i p_{ki}\Pr(s_{t-1}=i \mid y_{1:t-1})}$
with Kalman-filtered $f(y_t \mid s_t = j)$ per regime and a
mixing-collapse to keep the mixture from growing.

## Why it matters for the L2 project
If the L2 project ever needs a latent continuous state (e.g. a
time-varying Kyle lambda) under a discrete regime (macro Primed /
not), Kim's algorithm is the standard estimation machinery.
