## Summary

For a stationary Hawkes process λ(t) = μ + ∫φ(t−s)dN(s), the branching
ratio is n = ∫φ(τ)dτ (their Eq. 2 context: Λ = μ/(1−n)) — for the
exponential kernel φ = αe^{−βτ}, n = α/β, estimated as n̂ = α̂/β̂.
Hardiman & Bouchaud derive a MODEL-INDEPENDENT estimator from the
mean and variance of the event count N_W in a window W:
**n ≈ 1 − μ_W/σ²_W (their Eq. 7)**, exact as W → ∞, requiring only
W ≫ the correlation time and T ≫ W. Applying it to S&P e-mini mid-price
changes, they support the reading that the market is near-critical
(n → 1), and — the sharper point — that exponential-kernel MLE fits can
misreport long-memory (power-law kernel) data as criticality.

## What it gives the programme — five items

**H1 — The free, independent cross-check on every Hawkes fit.** Eq. 7
needs only the mean and variance of event counts — no kernel, no MLE.
Every branching ratio our `fit_hawkes2` reports (the verified estimator,
G = α/β) can be cross-checked on the same sample by the mean-variance
estimator at zero model risk. Disagreement beyond the known bias is a
defect alarm, not a finding. This belongs in the C1-L2/C8 diagnostics as
a standing report line, not a kill line — it is model-independent but
assumes stationarity, which a 24h crypto session is not.

**H2 — The bias direction is one-sided and known.** σ²_W/W is a biased
estimator of the autocovariance integral (their Eq. 5-6): a finite W
truncates the kernel's support, so **Eq. 7 systematically
UNDER-estimates n**, exact only as W → ∞. The comparison protocol must
remember the direction: mean-variance branching BELOW MLE branching is
the expected geometry; mean-variance branching ABOVE MLE branching is
the red flag. (Their Fig. 1: the underestimation is worst at large n —
exactly the region the C8 trigger cares about.)

**H3 — Never average branching-ratio estimates.** For finite samples
the mean of ñ over realizations is **−∞** (σ̃²_W = 0 has non-zero
probability); they report the **median**. Sibling-of-D5 in spirit: a
silent statistical convention (averaging across folds/windows) can
destroy an estimator. Any aggregation of per-window branching estimates
in our pipeline must use medians, and this should be asserted in code.

**H4 — Criticality is where estimation error explodes, and the C8
threshold sits there.** As n → 1, Λ = μ/(1−n) amplifies estimation error
in n̂ = α̂/β̂; the 0.8 trigger threshold is inside the region where
their Fig. 1 shows the estimator's worst behavior. The C8 trigger
therefore needs a pre-declared uncertainty band on the branching ratio
itself (bootstrap over windows, median aggregation per H3) — a trigger
that fires on a point estimate near 0.8 is a trigger that fires on noise
in exactly the way D5 would have manufactured.

**H5 — The kernel-misspecification control (the deepest item).** Their
sharpest result: a near-critical exponential-kernel fit can be an
ARTIFACT — data generated by a slowly-decaying (power-law) kernel fits
as n → 1 with an exponential kernel. Before any "the market is critical
/ reflexivity is rising" reading of C8's trigger, the kernel family
question must be pre-declared: fit both exponential and power-law
kernels, and if the power-law fit removes the criticality, the
criticality was misspecification, not the market. **Recommended as a
C8 pre-condition, written before any L2 data arrives:** no criticality
claim from an exponential-kernel fit alone.
