A SUPERPROCESS-BASED APPROACH TO ROUGH CIR PROCESSES AND FELLER
RANDOM MEASURES
GORDAN ŽITKOVIĆ
Abstract. Feller random measures generalize the Feller diffusion (the CIR process) by giving it mem-
ory. They arise as the scaling limits of nearly unstable Hawkes processes, and include the rough CIR
process and its hyper-rough and discontinuous relatives. We show that every Feller random measure
is the occupation measure of a Dawson–Watanabe superprocess whose spatial motion is a killed Lévy
subordinator, integrated over the branching time. This is a continuum analogue of the Hawkes–Oakes
cluster representation. It yields existence, stability in the parameters, and stochastic equations driven
by an explicit noise. The structure of this noise depends on whether the kernel has an atom at the
origin. Without an atom, the noise is a Brownian motion time-changed by the distribution function
of the measure. This leads to a martingale characterization and to sharp results on densities and their
Hölder regularity. With an atom, the noise is a compensated inverse-Gaussian process time-changed
by the compensator of that distribution function, and the measure is purely atomic.

2.1) plays the role of a memory kernel and µ ∈ Mlf (R+) describes “immigration”. Its law is given

The central observation of this paper is that Feller random measures admit a continuum analogue
of the Hawkes-Oakes cluster representation (Theorem 3.1), which makes them a natural continuous
generalization of Hawkes processes. The family is also affine: its log-Laplace functional is linear in
µ, with slope given by the solution of the Riccati-Volterra equation above. Consequently, it retains
the branching property of the Feller diffusion. As we show in this paper, the law of a Feller random
measure depends continuously on (ρ, µ). The family accommodates a wide range of self-exciting
behavior, with roughness and memory decoupled. Its members range from measures with Hölder-
continuous densities, through diffuse measures without a square-integrable density, to purely atomic
ones.

1.3. Main results. Besides the superprocess representation itself, the main new results are (i) ex-
istence for every PGID ρ, including ρ({0}) > 0, together with stability in (ρ, µ), which recovers,
for instance, the limit of the integrated variance in [AJAR26] as H ↘ −1/2; (ii) the structure of
the noise, which is determined by ρ and is inverse-Gaussian when ρ({0}) > 0; (iii) the “only if”
half of the density criterion (see subsubsection 1.3.4 below); and (iv) the exact Hölder exponent and
the failure of the semimartingale property of the density. The construction also gives new proofs of
several known results, often under weaker assumptions.
What follows is a more detailed list with attributions and comparisons with existing results.
1.3.1. Existence, uniqueness and stability. We show that every PGID ρ and every µ ∈ Mlf (R+)
define a Feller random measure, constructed directly from the superprocess (Corollary 3.4). The
solution v in Definition 2.2 is unique for every PGID ρ, including the discontinuous case (Proposition
2.4), so the law of ξ is determined by (ρ, µ). In [PŽ25], existence was obtained only for ρ({0}) = 0
and only as a limit of Hawkes processes. [Xu24, Theorem 2.9] also obtains these objects as limits of
Hawkes processes, including kernels with an atom at the origin, with convergence in the S topology.
In the univariate case, the kernels of [Xu24] are the measures with Laplace transform 1/Φ for an
extended Bernstein function Φ, i.e., a Bernstein function plus a real constant. When Φ(0) > 0 they
are, up to a constant factor, exactly the PGID laws. The kernels with Φ(0) ≤ 0 have infinite mass
and lie outside the present setting. Moreover, we show that the law of ξ depends continuously on
(ρ, µ) (Proposition 3.6), and also provide a vague version which accounts for kernel mass escaping
to infinity (Corollary 3.7).

1.3.4. Densities. Next, we prove that the measure ξ has a square-integrable density Z, i.e., one that
is square integrable on [0, T ] × Ω for each T , if and only if ρ has a locally square-integrable density
r (Proposition 4.6). The “if” half is close to [Xu24, Corollary 2.16] (see Remark 4.7). For fractional
kernels with H ∈ (−1/2, 0], [JR20, Theorem 3.1] show that ξ[0, ·] is not continuously differentiable.
The “only if” half shows, for general ρ, that there is not even a square-integrable density. The density
Z satisfies the stochastic equations of Theorem 4.8, which reduce to those of [JR16, EER19] in the
rough CIR case. At the critical index H = 0, Example 4.24 shows that a slowly varying correction
to the jump measure restores a square-integrable density, in line with the log-modulated models of
[BHP21], so that the Blumenthal-Getoor index of Π alone does not settle the question in general.
1.3.5. Regularity. The growth of Ψ at infinity controls the Hölder regularity of Z (Corollary 4.13),
which recovers the known Hölder continuity of the rough CIR process [AJLP19]. For b = 0, jump
densities comparable to t−1−α near the origin and bounded on [1, ∞), with α ∈ (1/2, 1), and
nonzero µ with a locally square-integrable density, we prove that the Hölder exponent α − 1/2 is
exact, and that no version of Z is a semimartingale (Propositions 4.17 and 4.18, Corollary 4.19). The
failure of the semimartingale property is often asserted, e.g., in [AJAR26, FGW25], and the optimality
of the Hölder exponent is implicit in the literature, but we have been unable to locate proofs of either.
The companion statement, the failure of the Markov property, is proved in [FGW25].
1.3.6. The discontinuous case. When ρ({0}) > 0, we prove that the noise N is a compensated
inverse-Gaussian process time-changed by the compensator of ξ[0, ·] (Theorem 5.1), and that ξ is
purely atomic (Corollary 5.4). For a given inverse-Gaussian subordinator, ξ[0, ·] is the unique so-
lution of a Volterra equation based on this description of its noise (Theorem 5.8). In [Xu24], these
objects appear as limits, together with a Volterra equation, but without a detailed description of its
martingale part (see Remark 5.9).

1.3.7. Examples. We provide several new examples, and establish novel properties of known ones.
Every Lévy subordinator gives an admissible kernel, with the behavior of Ψ at infinity setting the
roughness and around 0 the memory. In the continuous case, the examples of subsection 4.7 are the
classical CIR process (Example 4.20), the rough CIR family (Example 4.21), the critical index α = 1/2,
where the spatial motion is, up to a constant time change, the first-passage process of a Brownian
motion, and which connects with the catalytic construction (Example 4.23), log-modulated kernels
at the critical index (Example 4.24), and tempered kernels, which decouple roughness from memory
and correspond to the gamma kernel of [AJLP19] (Example 4.22). In the discontinuous case, we treat
ρ = δ0, for which ξ is independently scattered with inverse-Gaussian marginals (Example 5.10). For
Lebesgue µ, its distribution function is the inverse-Gaussian Lévy process obtained in [AJAR26] as
the limit of hyper-rough square-root processes as H ↘ −1/2. We recover this convergence of the
integrated variance, in the Skorokhod M1 topology, from Corollary 3.7. The joint convergence with
the martingale part, in the S topology, is not covered by our argument. We also treat lattice kernels,
which give exact inverse-Gaussian autoregressions (Examples 5.11 and 5.12). In the Poisson case,
the superprocess reduces to an infinite system of Feller-type stochastic differential equations, and
ξ is a Jiřina process [Jiř58] (Example 5.11). We also treat exponential excitation, which leads to a
continuous-state branching process with immigration (Example 5.13).

2.2. Feller random measures. The central object of this paper is the Feller random measure, in-
troduced in [PŽ25]. Feller random measures form a family of generalizations of the classical Feller
diffusion, indexed by a probability measure ρ on R+, which plays the role of a memory kernel, and a
measure µ, which describes immigration. For an exponential ρ one recovers the classical picture: the
(ρ, µ)-Feller random measure is ξ(dt) = Zt dt, where Z is a Feller diffusion with time-dependent
immigration given by µ. For a general ρ the memory is no longer exponential, and ξ may fail to
have a density altogether. Examples of concrete choices of ρ, and the random measures they give
rise to (the rough CIR process among them), are collected in subsection 4.7 and in Section 5, once
enough theory is available.
We start with the class of kernels required by the construction.
Definition 2.1 ([KMM84]). A probability measure ρ on R+ is called positive geometrically infin-
itely divisible (PGID) if, for every p ∈ (0, 1), it is the distribution of a sum of Gp nonnegative i.i.d.
random variables whose common law may depend on p, where Gp is independent of the summands
and geometric with parameter p, i.e., P[Gp = k] = p(1 − p)k−1 for k ≥ 1.
Feller random measures are defined by prescribing their Laplace functionals, with ρ entering as
the kernel of a Riccati-Volterra equation. Why ρ is required to be PGID, rather than an arbitrary
probability measure on R+, is explained in Remark 2.3(2) below.

5. The discontinuous case
In this section we treat the case when p := ρ({0}) > 0. According to Proposition 2.6, this is
equivalent to L being a compound Poisson process, i.e., to b = 0 and Π being a finite measure. The
structure of ξ and of its vertical noise N then differs from the continuous case: for µ ̸= 0, ξ is purely
atomic, and N is a time-changed compensated inverse-Gaussian process rather than a time-changed
Brownian motion.
Subsection 5.1 describes the noise as a time-changed compensated inverse-Gaussian process (The-

N as a Brownian motion time-changed by Γ (Corollary 5.6 and Remark 5.7). Subsection 5.2 derives
a stochastic Volterra equation for ξ[0, ·], driven by an inverse-Gaussian subordinator, and proves
uniqueness (Theorem 5.8). Subsection 5.3 collects examples.
