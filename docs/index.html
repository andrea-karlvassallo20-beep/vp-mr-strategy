Knowledge base → strategy design

The strategy is grounded in a 14-book study library. Each knowledge layer maps to a concrete design decision; candidate improvements become research variants that the edge-seeker must beat honestly — the deployed parameters stay frozen (Gate 3).

Elliott Wave (Balan)
Regimes: impulse vs corrective phases; mean revention works in ranges, fails in impulses
Motivates the z-score mean-reversion hypothesis AND its known failure mode; the walk-forward and deployment-envelope studies quantify when the edge exists
VSA — Master the Markets (Williams)
"Effort vs results"; weak-volume stretches lack real pressure
`VariantStrategy` volume filter: entries only when the stretch bar's volume is below its trailing mean — a research candidate, evaluated nightly
Harris, Trading & Exchanges
Microstructure frictions are a first-class cost
Module 4 friction model: half-spread, latency, commission in every backtest and paper fill
Hasbrouck / Biais-Glosten-Spatt
Efficient price vs microstructure noise
The MTM equity reconstruction separates position PnL from noise; the SPI audit verifies decision-time data
Bouchaud et al.
Square-root impact law
Our impact model is exactly sqrt-impact (`impact_coeff * sqrt(qty)`), aligned with the econophysics evidence
Stoll (2002)
Five sources of the spread; temporary vs permanent impact; effective vs realized spread
The expected-cost model charges the informed-flow tax explicitly; the paper sessions will compare expected vs realized costs (future refinement: asymmetric impact)
Madhavan (2000)
Information effects permanent, inventory effects transitory
Grounds the VSA filter: a low-volume stretch is more likely transitory (inventory/noise), a high-volume one more likely informed
Foucault-Pagano-Roell
Liquidity provision, fragmentation, transparency
Informs venue/broker assumptions in the run card; maker-taker economics noted for future live routing
Dark Pools (Patterson)
Feedback loops, quote withdrawal, HFT latency races
Latency modeled at 5ms; kill-switch and flatten-at-close exist precisely because mechanical markets can gap through stops
The Quants (Patterson)
Kelly genealogy; 1987 fat tails; funding spirals; model risk
0.25x fractional Kelly haircut, 0.5% per-trade risk cap, 2% daily kill-switch, 1.0x gross exposure ceiling — all conservative by design
Bjork
Martingales; falsification over curve-fitting; stops as hitting-probability bets
The walk-forward and Monte Carlo studies are falsification tests; the ATR trailing stop is treated as a hitting-probability exit, sized via the ATR-risk cap
Javaheri
Identification hierarchy: at daily frequency, noise params nearly unestimable
Justifies EWMA/GARCH(1,1) over Heston/3/2 in `vol_models.py`; no over-parameterized vol model at daily bars
Levy, Computational Finance
GARCH(1,1), persistence Sigma(alpha+beta)<1, EWMA lambda=0.97 (RiskMetrics), vol clustering
`ewma_vol_ann` (lambda=0.97) and `garch_vol_ann` (alpha=0.05, beta=0.90, persistence 0.95) as sizing-vol candidates in the edge-seeker grid

README — the project explained — README.md
# Salient — an autonomous quantitative trading system

**What this is:** a complete, self-running quantitative trading pipeline — a
mean-reversion strategy for US equity ETFs, wrapped in hard safety gates,
statistically validated, connected to a real execution engine, and currently
**paper-trading itself against live market data every night** — auditing its
own decisions, seeking a better edge, and reporting everything to a dashboard.

**The goal:** find the edge, through evidence, not enthusiasm. This is a
hobby research project — it will never trade live, and the enjoyment comes
from solving, not financial gain. The promotion gate still matters: the
system must complete 20 forward paper-trading sessions with a Signal Parity
Index >= 0.98, zero duplicate order fires, and zero risk-kill-switch trips —
and it judges itself. Beyond the deployed universe, a nightly cross-asset
research pass hunts for mean-reversion structure in crypto (BTC, ETH), main
indices (S&P 500, Nasdaq, Dow) and commodities (gold, crude, silver).

## The strategy in one paragraph
Each trading day, after the US market close, the strategy computes a 100-day
z-score of each ETF's price against its trailing window. When price stretches
2 standard deviations below the rolling mean (and the market is mean-reverting),
it buys; 2 above, it sells. Position size is volatility parity scaled by a
quarter-Kelly haircut, capped at 0.5% equity risk per trade. A 3x-ATR trailing
stop exits losers, a 2% daily drawdown kill-switch halts everything, and all
positions flatten at session close. Parameters are frozen at deployment.

## Why it can be trusted (the four safety gates)
| Gate | Guarantee |
|---|---|
| **G1 zero-lookahead** | Signals at bar t use only data <= t-1. Verified by future-perturbation audit: mutating future bars cannot change past decisions. |
| **G2 AST sandbox** | The strategy core imports only numpy/math/typing/dataclasses/enum. No OS, network, or eval — enforced by static analysis in CI. |
| **G3 parameter freezing** | Hyperparameters are immutable after deployment; mid-run re-optimization raises an exception. The edge-seeker may *propose* changes; only a human can apply them. |
| **G4 order state machine** | Every order walks an explicit state graph with illegal-transition guards and duplicate-fire protection. |

## The autonomous loop (runs nightly, 21:30 UTC Mon-Fri)
```
fetch real bars (SPY QQQ IWM XLF XLE)
  -> run the persisted paper session
  -> SPI audit (decision-time signal parity)
  -> performance report (MTM equity, Sharpe, drawdown)
  -> edge-seeker research pass (walk-forward candidate proposals)
  -> promotion gate verdict (PROMOTE / HOLD vs run-card criteria)
  -> regenerate dashboard + work log
  -> commit everything to main
```
