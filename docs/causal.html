Every Concept, Taught as Causal Chains.

Most people learn a research programme by collecting definitions and results. They recognize those facts in familiar questions but struggle when a test changes a variable, combines two mechanisms, or asks them to predict an unfamiliar consequence. This page teaches the programme's thirty concepts differently: each one becomes a sequence of causal inevitabilities. Every link states the starting condition, the consequence that must follow from it, and the contradiction or failure that would appear if it did not follow. If you can state what would break, you understand the mechanism; if you cannot, you have memorized a formula.

Every link carries a source audit, because the programme's epistemic contract is the audit:

Part 1 — The market concepts (1–10)

1. Volatility clustering — and why it mean-reverts

Because volatility is the amplitude of arriving order flow, and flow arrives in episodes (news, liquidations, cascades), today's amplitude must predict tomorrow's. Otherwise markets would flip between calm and crash with no persistence, and every options desk would be pricing an unpriceable vega book. INFERRED

Because episodes end, the elevated amplitude must decay once the episode passes. Otherwise volatility would ratchet upward forever and no market would ever quiet down — yet the board's coils (KAVA 41 days, HOLO 70) are exactly this decay, measured. GROUNDED

2. Heavy tails — why thresholds live in sigma units

Because price is the sum of bursty order flow, return distributions must have tails heavier than the Gaussian. Otherwise a 9.95σ single day (TIA, before its collapse) would be a once-in-1022 impossibility — yet it happened, and the census found 37.5% of crypto coil exits contain a >4σ day. GROUNDED

Because tails are heavy, a move threshold must be stated in units of the asset's own recent volatility. Otherwise the same 5% means nothing to BTC and everything to a memecoin, and every cross-sectional test silently compares incomparable things. INFERRED

3. Autocorrelation with horizon structure

Because at short horizons liquidity provision dominates (impact, then reversion) and at long horizons persistent flows dominate, the sign of autocorrelation must depend on the horizon. Otherwise trend-following and mean-reversion could not both survive in the same asset — yet they do. INFERRED

4. Concave impact — the square-root law

Because each incremental unit of size must reach progressively less patient holders, impact must grow with the square root of size, not linearly. Otherwise a large order would move price proportionally to its size, no institution could ever trade, and effort-vs-result (volume without displacement) could not exist as an absorption signature. INFERRED

5. Self-excitation — events beget events

Because one large order splits into many (execution) and attracts followers, event arrivals must cluster beyond Poisson. Otherwise the Hawkes branching measured in the dry run would sit at zero and the C8 trigger signature ("excitation going critical") would describe nothing. GROUNDED

Because self-excitation is multiplicative but bounded, the branching ratio must stay below one. Otherwise the process explodes — activity feeding activity forever, which no market does. INFERRED

6. Edge decay

Because any measurable advantage induces others to trade it, the advantage must erode. Otherwise edges would persist indefinitely, compounding would make every discoverer infinitely rich, and falsification would be pointless. INFERRED

Because edges decay, a result must be forward-scored before it is believed. Otherwise the design-sample trap (the old v4 page's simulated long rule: +34.8% in-sample) would pass as an edge — it stays flagged forever. GROUNDED

7. Compression and the salience gradient — the coiled spring

Because a deep coil means the marginal seller at the lows has been exhausted while the asset leaves the attention of the marginal buyer, a coiled-and-forgotten market must carry a higher conditional probability of a significant move. Otherwise C7's primed realization rate would equal its matched quiet-but-not-coiled controls — it does not: 0.940 vs 0.862, +7.9pp, p<0.001 Bonferroni. GROUNDED

Because the signature is about attention, it must concentrate at the range bottom. Otherwise the census would not find 72.3% of big crypto up-moves starting in the bottom quarter of the range. GROUNDED

8. The certainty–timing–direction triangle

Because certainty costs time (you must wait for the coil) and direction is not measurable from price, you can buy at most two corners of the triangle. Otherwise a strategy claiming all three would be claiming a free lunch, and the programme's refusal to name a direction would be a stylistic choice instead of a measurement boundary. GROUNDED

9. The 0.505 boundary — direction, refused by measurement

Because daily price-derived features carry no directional information in this record (P(up) = 0.505, CI [0.497, 0.513], n = 15,386), direction must never be claimed from them. Otherwise the interval would sit away from 0.50 — it does not, and every claim that ignores this is a coin flip in a costume. GROUNDED

10. The volatility clock — the demoted but real finding

Because compression adds nothing beyond the volatility it is built from at the daily scale (the vol-tautology kill, 4/4), the daily hazard layer must be demoted to a descriptive clock. Otherwise the block-temporal placebo and the scale-invariance null would not both have fired — they did, and C1's death record is the proof that demotion was honest. GROUNDED

Part 2 — The statistical machinery (11–22)

11. Conditional probability and confidence intervals

Because every number here is computed from a finite sample, every estimate must carry its interval. Otherwise a single run is treated as truth — and the C7 incident (a false kill from comparing two intervals to each other instead of testing the difference against zero) would have been a real kill, recorded in the code forever. GROUNDED

12. Hazard and survival — the mathematics of “when”

Because the question is when an event occurs and many series have not finished yet, the event must be modeled as a hazard with censoring. Otherwise every unfinished observation is thrown away, and the long tail of “not yet” — the majority of the data — is discarded. INFERRED

13. Triple-barrier labels — defining the event without hindsight

Because the label must be fixed before the test, the event must be defined by pre-declared barriers (return, time, volatility). Otherwise hindsight selects the window, and everything passes — which is why C7's label was registered in the spec before the single legal run. GROUNDED

14. The Cox model

Because several covariates may shift the hazard simultaneously, they must enter one proportional-hazards regression. Otherwise each covariate needs its own experiment, the sample fragments, and the interactions (the C8 claim: primed × microstructure) can never be asked. INFERRED

15. Purged cross-validation and embargo

Because label horizons overlap fold boundaries, the future must be purged and embargoed out of every training set. Otherwise the model grades its own homework, and the leakage assertion — the exact complement of the purge condition, enforced in code — would be violable. It is not. GROUNDED

16. Placebo tests

Because a dead market produces patterns by construction, any claimed mechanism must survive a placebo that should show nothing. Otherwise the C1 block-temporal placebo would not have fired 4/4 and killed the daily layer — it did, and that is what a placebo is for. GROUNDED

17. The scale-invariance null

Because a real mechanism survives rescaling of units, the null must fail to reject under rescaling. Otherwise the “finding” is an artifact of the ruler — C1 fired this null 4/4, and the layer died on it. GROUNDED

18. Vol-tautology

Because “compression” is built from volatility, any compression claim must show value beyond quiet itself. Otherwise primed is just quiet wearing a costume — which is why C7's third kill line tests primed-minus-control against zero, and why the coil answered: +7.9pp beyond matched quiet. GROUNDED

19. Concordance

Because a timing model is an ordering claim, it must be graded on the ordering (concordance), not on one lucky call. Otherwise a single correct prediction is indistinguishable from skill — and the daily layer's death record shows what grading honestly does to luck. INFERRED

20. The Deflated Sharpe Ratio

Because trying many things finds things by construction, the Sharpe of the best of N must be deflated by the number of trials. Otherwise the everything-search turns noise into an “edge” — precisely what the multiplicity caveat binds against on every scan in the dossier. INFERRED

21. Brier score and climatology

Because a probability is only meaningful against the base rate, it must be graded by Brier against climatology. Otherwise “I said 60%” is unfalsifiable — and C7's climatology (0.862) is what makes the primed rate (0.940) a number instead of a boast. GROUNDED

22. Risk-reward, drawdown, and why RR ∼1.4 is not enough

Because the 86% baseline event forbids holding premium (theta on a 12-week validity window), the position must be sized to the lower bound of the hazard-lift distribution under quarter-Kelly and CVaR. Otherwise the premium bleeds the account while waiting — and the refusal rule (trigger lift straddles 1.0 → position zero) would have no reason to exist. GROUNDED

Part 3 — The microstructure concepts (23–30)

23. The limit order book — where prices actually come from

Because price is the point where the marginal buyer meets the marginal seller, the book is the market. Otherwise there is no spread, no impact, and nothing for the verification gate to check — yet mid agreement and depth consistency are exactly what the gate measures before anything else runs. GROUNDED

24. Order flow and the aggressor

Because every trade crossed the spread, someone chose urgency; the aggressor flag must carry that choice. Otherwise buying pressure and selling pressure are indistinguishable — and the binary int8 aggressor column, with its coercion-failure-kills-the-file rule, would encode nothing. GROUNDED

25. VPIN — a toxicity gauge on trade buckets

Because informed flow arrives as persistent one-sided trade bursts, bulk-classified imbalance must anticipate toxicity. Otherwise the VPIN spike measured on real BTCUSDT aggTrades across the Aug 2024 carry-unwind window is noise — the dry run recorded it as a validated signal, not a claim. GROUNDED

26. Hawkes processes

Because self-excitation has a measurable spectral radius, the branching ratio must be estimable and bounded. Otherwise “Hawkes excitation going critical” — the first pre-declared C8 trigger signature — would be poetry instead of a threshold. INFERRED

27. OFI — order flow imbalance at the edge of the book

Because the best bid and ask move when flow at the edge is one-sided, OFI must predict short-horizon returns. Otherwise the book would be static and the second trigger signature (OFI absorption with declining Kyle lambda) would describe nothing. UNSUPPORTED on real books — bookTicker is not publicly archived; validated on synthetic frames only, and the programme says so.

28. Kyle's lambda and liquidity

Because impact per unit flow is the price of immediacy, lambda must measure liquidity. Otherwise absorption — volume without displacement, the effort-vs-result reading — cannot be distinguished from a book that simply isn't there. INFERRED

29. Event clock vs wall clock

Because the market experiences events, not seconds, features must be computed on the event clock (state-change rows, self-thinning). Otherwise dead hours get equal weight against the crash minutes, and every microstructure feature is diluted by clock time the market never lived. GROUNDED

30. Cancellation vs trade

Because a cancellation moves the book without printing a trade, the two must be distinguished in the data. Otherwise OFI computed on trades alone misreads flicker as flow — which is why the verification gate's sequence-gap detection (U/u/pu event codes) runs first, and nothing downstream runs before it passes. GROUNDED
